# Peacewright Project

> **Peacemaking is something you do. A peacemaker is someone who keeps choosing to do it.**

Peacewright is an open project exploring a deceptively simple question:

**What does it mean to make peace?**

The project begins with a phrase attributed to Jesus in the Gospel of Matthew:

> “Blessed are the peacemakers.”

That phrase is our starting point, not our boundary.

The idea of making peace belongs to no single religion, philosophy, culture, nation, or political tradition. Peacewright explores how people across different traditions and circumstances have understood peace, conflict, reconciliation, justice, compassion, resistance, cooperation, and the work of building something better.

## Peacemaking Is Something We Do

Peacewright does not treat *peacemaker* as a permanent label bestowed on certain kinds of people.

A teacher is not automatically a peacemaker because they teach.

A doctor is not automatically a peacemaker because they heal.

A religious leader, activist, diplomat, soldier, parent, artist, tradesperson, or politician is not automatically a peacemaker because of their title.

Peacemaking happens through choices and actions.

It can be planned or spontaneous. It can happen between nations, within communities, across a dinner table, at work, between strangers, or inside a damaged relationship.

Sometimes it may look like reconciliation. Sometimes service. Sometimes courage. Sometimes restraint. Sometimes resistance to injustice.

And sometimes reasonable people will disagree about whether an action made peace at all.

Those disagreements are part of what Peacewright exists to explore.

## Peace Has More Than One Vocabulary

Peacewright began with the Greek word **εἰρηνοποιός (*eirēnopoios*)**, commonly translated *peacemaker*.

But that is only one doorway.

Human cultures and traditions have developed rich concepts that can illuminate different dimensions of peacemaking, including ideas such as:

- **Shalom**
- **Ahimsa**
- **Mettā / Maitrī**
- **Hózhó**
- **Sarbat da Bhala**
- **K’é**
- **The Haudenosaunee Great Law of Peace**
- **εἰρηνοποιός (*eirēnopoios*)**

We want to understand these ideas on their own terms before trying to combine them into a universal theory.

That means studying their languages, histories, cultures, tensions, limitations, and lived expressions—and being careful not to claim connections or influences where the evidence does not support them.

There will be more.

## Peacewright Is Not a Religious Project

Christianity does not own peacemaking.

Peacewright is not a Christian ministry, church, religious movement, or attempt to convert anyone.

We can take Jesus seriously without requiring someone to be Christian.

We can study a Jewish concept without becoming Judaism.

We can learn from Buddhist, Hindu, Sikh, Indigenous, secular, philosophical, historical, and other traditions without pretending they all say the same thing.

They don't.

That difference is valuable.

## Why "Peacewright"?

A *wright* is a maker.

Shipwrights make ships. Wheelwrights make wheels. Playwrights make plays.

A **peacewright** makes peace.

The name reflects one of the project's central ideas: peace is not merely something we hope for, believe in, or describe.

**Peace is something people make.**

Making also implies work.

Things that are made require judgment, skill, practice, tools, experimentation, repair, and sometimes starting over.

Peacemaking may be much the same.

## This Project Is Meant to Change

This repository is not intended to become a finished manifesto carved in stone.

It is a working record of an evolving investigation.

Ideas will be proposed. Definitions will change. Research will challenge assumptions. People will disagree. Mistakes will be corrected. New traditions and perspectives will be added.

GitHub gives us a way to make that evolution visible.

Rather than quietly rewriting our history, we can preserve it.

## Where We're Going

Peacewright is intended to grow beyond this repository.

The long-term vision includes public conversations, educational material, social media, research, stories of everyday peacemaking, community participation, and eventually tools that help people think through conflicts and discover practical ways to make peace in their own lives and communities.

But the ideas come first.

This repository is where we build them.

## Current Status

**Early development.**

Peacewright is still defining its foundations. The documents in this repository should be understood as working material rather than final doctrine.

The project will eventually establish clearer processes for contributions, sourcing, review, versioning, governance, and licensing.

For now, we're building in public.
